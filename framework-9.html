Financial Health Analysis

Cash Flow, Profitability, and Long-Term Sustainability

← Back to All Frameworks

The Challenge

Many businesses fail not because they don't have customers or generate revenue, but because they don't manage cash and profitability well. Financial health determines whether a business can invest in growth, weather downturns, and ultimately survive and thrive long-term. This framework provides clarity on financial performance and decisions.

Financial Health Components

1. Profitability Analysis

Are we making money, and where is profit (or loss) coming from?

  • Gross profit: Revenue - COGS (shows product profitability)
  • Operating profit: Gross profit - Operating expenses (shows business efficiency)
  • Net profit: Operating profit - Interest/taxes (bottom line profit)
  • Margin analysis: Gross %, operating %, net % (shows profitability at each level)
  • Profitability by segment: Which products/customers/markets are profitable?

Key insight: High revenue does not equal profitability. Some of the most profitable businesses have modest revenue.

2. Cash Flow Management

Do we have cash when we need it, even if profitable?

Cash flow types:

  • Operating cash flow: Cash generated by normal business operations
  • Investing cash flow: Cash used to buy assets or invest in growth
  • Financing cash flow: Cash from borrowing or investments

Why it matters: Even profitable businesses can fail if they run out of cash. Manage working capital carefully.

  • Accounts receivable—faster payment from customers improves cash
  • Inventory—less inventory ties up less cash
  • Accounts payable—slower payment to suppliers preserves cash

3. Balance Sheet Health

What are our assets, liabilities, and equity?

  • Liquidity: Do we have enough cash/current assets to cover short-term obligations?
  • Debt levels: How much leverage do we have? (debt/equity ratio)
  • Asset quality: Are our assets productive or obsolete?
  • Working capital: Current assets - current liabilities (ability to operate)

Health indicators: Strong liquidity, manageable debt, productive assets = financial strength.

4. Growth Metrics

Is the business growing, and at a sustainable rate?

  • Revenue growth: Year-over-year revenue increase
  • Profit growth: Is profit growing faster or slower than revenue?
  • Unit economics: Cost per unit, revenue per unit
  • Organic vs. inorganic: Growth from operations vs. acquisition
  • Growth rate sustainability: Can we maintain this growth rate?

Growth paradox: Fast growth is good, but not if it's destroying profitability or cash flow.

5. Return Metrics (ROI, ROE, ROIC)

How much return are we generating on invested capital?

  • ROI: Net profit / Investment (what return are we generating?)
  • ROE: Net profit / Equity (return to shareholders)
  • ROIC: NOPAT / Invested capital (return on all capital invested)
  • Benchmark: Compare to cost of capital; ROIC > Cost of capital = value creation

6. Financial Planning & Forecasting

Can we predict our financial future and plan accordingly?

  • Annual budget—what expenses are planned?
  • Revenue forecast—realistic projection based on pipeline and trends?
  • Cash flow forecast—will we have enough cash?
  • Scenario planning—what happens under different conditions?
  • Capital needs—when will we need to raise money?

Best practice: Monthly monitoring of actual vs. plan; quarterly forecast updates.

7. Financial Risk Management

What financial risks could threaten the business?

  • Revenue risk: Loss of major customer or market downturn
  • Cost risk: Inflation, input cost increases
  • Liquidity risk: Inability to access cash when needed
  • Currency risk: For international business
  • Interest rate risk: For businesses with variable-rate debt

Mitigation: Diversify revenue, manage debt levels, maintain cash reserves, build flexibility.

Financial Health Dashboard

Monitor these key metrics to assess financial health:

  • Gross profit margin and trend
  • Operating profit margin and trend
  • Cash balance and cash flow forecast
  • Revenue growth rate
  • Days sales outstanding (invoice collection speed)
  • Debt-to-equity ratio
  • Current ratio (liquidity)
  • Burn rate (if unprofitable) or cash generation

Strengthen Your Financial Foundation

Financial health enables strategy execution. Poor financial management constrains options and creates existential risk.
