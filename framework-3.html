Market Analysis Framework

Evaluating Market Opportunity, Competition, and Strategic Positioning

← Back to All Frameworks

The Challenge

Understanding market dynamics is critical for business success. Is the market growing or shrinking? Who are the competitors? What's our competitive advantage? Without a clear market analysis, businesses often overestimate opportunities, underestimate threats, and make poor strategic decisions.

Market Analysis Components

1. Market Size & Growth

How big is the opportunity, and is it growing?

Understanding the total addressable market (TAM), serviceable addressable market (SAM), and growth trajectory determines whether the market is worth pursuing.

Key metrics:

  • Total Addressable Market (TAM): Entire possible market for the solution
  • Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM): Market we can realistically reach
  • Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM): Market share we can capture
  • Historical growth rate: Is market growing or declining?
  • Projected growth: Future growth potential

2. Customer Needs Analysis

What problems are customers trying to solve?

Understanding the underlying customer needs and pain points reveals whether there's real demand and how we can create value.

Analysis techniques:

  • Problem validation: Is this a real problem worth solving?
  • Pain intensity: How much does it cost customers to live with this problem?
  • Purchase drivers: What motivates buying decision?
  • Switching costs: Are customers sticky or easily swayed?
  • Willingness to pay: What price would they accept?

3. Competitive Landscape

Who are we competing against, and what are they good at?

Competitive analysis reveals threats, opportunities for differentiation, and where to position ourselves in the market.

Competitive types:

  • Direct competitors: Solve the same problem the same way
  • Indirect competitors: Solve the same problem differently
  • Substitute competitors: Different approach to same underlying need
  • New entrants: Startups or new players entering the market

4. Competitive Positioning (Porter's Five Forces)

What are the structural features of market competition?

  • Bargaining power of buyers: Can customers strong-arm providers on price/terms?
  • Bargaining power of suppliers: Do suppliers control margins through scarcity?
  • Threat of new entrants: How easy is it for new competitors to enter?
  • Threat of substitutes: Can customers solve the problem another way?
  • Competitive rivalry: How intense is competition among existing players?

Market attractiveness: Industries with low buyer/supplier power, high barriers to entry, few substitutes, and moderate rivalry are most profitable.

5. Industry Dynamics & Trends

What forces are shaping the market?

Market forces like technology, regulation, consumer behavior, and economics create both threats and opportunities.

Key trends to monitor:

  • Technology disruption: Digital transformation, AI, automation
  • Regulatory changes: New rules that benefit or hurt competition
  • Consolidation: Industry mergers creating scale or barriers
  • Customer behavior shifts: Preference changes, new channels
  • Economic cycles: Recessions, inflation, currency movements

6. Competitive Differentiation

What makes us different and defensible?

Competitive advantage comes from doing something competitors can't replicate easily or affordably.

Types of differentiation:

  • Cost leadership: Lowest cost producer
  • Differentiation: Unique features or superior quality
  • Brand/customer relationships: Loyalty and preference
  • Technology/IP: Patents, algorithms, proprietary methods
  • Network effects: Value increases with more users

7. Market Entry Strategy

How do we establish market position?

  • Niche first: Own a small segment, then expand
  • Direct assault: Go head-to-head with established players
  • Partnership: Work with existing players rather than compete
  • Adjacent expansion: Enter from related market where we're strong

Critical Questions for Market Analysis

  • Is the market large enough and growing to justify investment?
  • Do real customer needs exist, or are we creating demand?
  • Who are the 3-5 key competitors, and what are their strengths/weaknesses?
  • What's our sustainable competitive advantage?
  • How easily can competitors replicate our differentiation?
  • What market trends should we be worried about?
  • What's our realistic market share potential?
