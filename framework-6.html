Cost Structure Analysis

Controlling Costs and Protecting Profitability

← Back to All Frameworks

The Challenge

Revenue alone doesn't determine profitability. Two businesses with identical revenue can have vastly different profitability based on their cost structure. Controlling costs is about making strategic decisions about where to spend, where to optimize, and where to invest for future growth. This framework helps analyze and optimize cost structure for maximum profitability.

Cost Structure Components

1. Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)

How much does it cost to produce/deliver what we sell?

COGS includes all direct costs of producing the product or service. Gross margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue.

  • Raw materials/inventory
  • Manufacturing/production labor
  • Fulfillment and shipping
  • Third-party services used to deliver

Optimization strategies: Volume discounts, vendor negotiation, process efficiency, automation, outsourcing.

2. Operating Expenses (OpEx)

What does it cost to run the business?

Major OpEx categories:

  • Sales & Marketing: Salaries, advertising, events, tools
  • Technology: Software, cloud infrastructure, development
  • People: G&A salaries, HR, finance, legal
  • Facilities: Rent, utilities, office equipment
  • Professional services: Consulting, accounting, legal

Key metric: OpEx as % of revenue shows efficiency (lower = better, but must maintain quality).

3. Fixed vs. Variable Costs

Do costs change with volume, or are they constant?

  • Fixed costs: Don't change with volume (rent, salaries, insurance)
  • Variable costs: Scale with volume (materials, shipping, transaction fees)
  • Leverage point: Higher fixed costs = higher profit margins at scale, but more risk at low volume

Example: Software company has high fixed R&D but near-zero COGS per customer = huge margins as scale grows. Restaurant has high variable costs = lower margins even at scale.

4. Cost Analysis & Segmentation

Which products/customers are most profitable?

  • Calculate profitability by product/service line
  • Identify low-margin, high-volume items vs. high-margin items
  • Analyze customer profitability (some cost more to serve)
  • Look for opportunities to cut low-margin business
  • Identify products with scale potential

Strategic insight: 80/20 rule often applies—20% of products generate 80% of profit. Focus there.

5. Cost Control Levers

Where can we reduce costs without hurting business?

  • Reduce waste: Eliminate unnecessary spending, consolidate vendors
  • Improve efficiency: Process improvements, automation, better scheduling
  • Renegotiate: Better supplier deals, service contracts
  • Outsource: Move non-core activities to specialists
  • Right-size: Eliminate underutilized assets or teams
  • Technology investment: Automate to reduce long-term labor

Caution: Cost-cutting shouldn't compromise quality or growth potential. It should be strategic, not across-the-board.

6. Economies of Scale

How do costs improve as we grow?

  • Volume discounts on inputs reduce COGS
  • Fixed costs spread across more units improves unit economics
  • Operational efficiencies emerge at scale
  • Margin expansion as business grows (path to profitability)

Example: Startup might have 30% gross margin due to small volume. At 10x scale, same product might achieve 55% margin through volume discounts and efficiency.

7. Cost Structure Targets & Benchmarks

What's healthy for our industry?

  • Industry varies widely (retail 20-35% margin, SaaS 70-80% gross margin)
  • Gross margin target: Enough to cover OpEx and maintain profit
  • Operating margin target: Net operating profit after OpEx
  • EBITDA margin target: Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization
  • Research peer performance to understand competitive positioning

Cost Structure Optimization Process

  1. Understand current state: Detailed breakdown of all costs
  2. Benchmark: Compare to industry peers and best practices
  3. Identify gaps: Where is our cost structure inefficient?
  4. Prioritize opportunities: Which improvements have biggest impact?
  5. Implement: Execute changes while managing risk
  6. Monitor: Track results and course-correct
