Business Model Analysis

Understanding How Businesses Create, Deliver, and Capture Value

← Back to All Frameworks

The Challenge

Many businesses struggle because they don't have clarity on how they actually make money, who they serve, and what makes them different. A business model describes the logic of how a company creates, delivers, and captures value. Without understanding this model, strategic decisions become unfocused and growth becomes difficult.

The Core Components

1. Value Proposition

What unique value do we deliver?

The specific benefits customers receive that they can't get elsewhere. This goes beyond features to outcomes and emotional benefits.

Examples:

  • Premium quality at luxury price point (e.g., high-end fashion)
  • Superior convenience (e.g., one-day delivery)
  • Best cost (e.g., fast fashion, discount retailers)
  • Exceptional customer service (e.g., luxury hotels)
  • Innovation and cutting-edge features (e.g., tech startups)

2. Customer Segments

Who do we serve, and how are they different?

Different customer types have different needs, buying behaviors, and profitability. Understanding these segments shapes product, pricing, and go-to-market strategies.

Segmentation dimensions:

  • Demographics (age, income, education, location)
  • Psychographics (values, lifestyle, attitudes)
  • Behavioral (purchase frequency, loyalty, usage patterns)
  • Industry/Company size (B2B: enterprise vs. SME vs. startup)
  • Use cases (how and why they use the product)

3. Distribution Channels

How do we reach and serve customers?

The pathways through which customers discover, purchase, and receive products/services. Channel choice impacts customer experience, cost, and scalability.

Channel types:

  • Direct: Company-owned retail stores, e-commerce site, sales team
  • Indirect: Third-party retailers, distributors, resellers
  • Digital: Marketplaces, apps, social commerce
  • Hybrid: Mix of owned and partnered channels

4. Revenue Streams

How do we make money from each customer segment?

The different ways the business generates income. Most businesses have multiple revenue streams with different margins, growth rates, and risk profiles.

Revenue stream types:

  • Transaction-based: One-time purchase
  • Subscription: Recurring monthly/annual payments
  • Usage-based: Pay per unit used (e.g., cloud computing)
  • Licensing: Fees for intellectual property use
  • Freemium: Free base product, paid premium features
  • Multi-sided: Revenue from different customer types (e.g., ads + subscriptions)

5. Key Resources

What assets must we have to deliver value?

The critical resources needed to operate the business model. These differ by business type and become strategic advantages when rare or difficult to replicate.

Resource categories:

  • Physical: Factories, distribution centers, stores, vehicles
  • Intellectual: Patents, brands, databases, algorithms
  • Human: Specialized talent, expertise, relationships
  • Financial: Capital, credit lines, investor relationships

6. Key Partnerships

Who do we work with to enable our business model?

Strategic relationships that extend capabilities, reduce risk, or accelerate growth. Partnerships can address resource gaps or create competitive advantages.

Partnership types:

  • Suppliers: Resource providers
  • Distributors: Channel partners
  • Technology partners: Enable innovation or scale
  • Complementary businesses: Services sold together
  • Strategic alliances: Long-term competitive positioning

7. Cost Structure

What are the major costs to operate this model?

Understanding cost structure reveals profitability potential and cost control opportunities. High fixed vs. variable cost models have different operating dynamics.

Cost categories:

  • Cost of goods sold (COGS): Manufacturing, sourcing
  • Operating expenses: People, rent, utilities
  • Technology/infrastructure: Systems, platforms
  • Sales and marketing: Customer acquisition
  • R&D: Product development and innovation

Analysis Questions

When analyzing a business model, ask:

  • Is the value proposition compelling and defensible?
  • Are we focused on the most attractive customer segments?
  • Are our channels cost-effective and reaching the right people?
  • Do our revenue streams align with customer willingness to pay?
  • Do we have or can we acquire the key resources we need?
  • Are our partnerships strategic and sustainable?
  • Is our cost structure sustainable and profitable at scale?
